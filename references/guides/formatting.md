# Formatting guide

Each specialist gets only its own guide. Examples are invented to illustrate each principle; never reuse them in the writer's script. [general] marks craft-book knowledge.

Points marked [general] come from standard industry format references and Final Draft/WGA practice.

## Principles and examples

**1. One consistent heading pattern.** INT./EXT. – LOCATION – TIME. If the film tracks time of day, use specific times consistently. Sub-locations after a dash. [general]
> "EXT. GRANTON - HIGH STREET - CONTINUOUS"

**2. Caps for a character's name at first appearance in the action, with an age.** A dialogue cue (even O.S.) doesn't count as an introduction. [general]
> "RAFI (38) drops the keys twice before the lock turns."

**3. Extensions say where the voice comes from.** (V.O.) not in the scene; (O.S.) in the location but off screen. A constant non-physical voice (an AI, a voice heard throughout on a device) can take plain cues. [general]

**4. Parentheticals short and rare, never doing a stage direction's job.**
> "It's me. Owen. / (beat) / Your brother."

**5. Transitions sparingly.** A new heading already implies a cut. [general]

**6. Label montages; set up intercuts once.**
> MONTAGE - JOB HUNT / - Applications stacked on a kitchen table (Night) [general format example]

**7. Screen and phone text word for word, with one consistent label style** (ON THE PHONE / ON THE LAPTOP). Avoid double colons. Quote exact on-screen wording.
> "June's phone buzzes. It reads: 'Landlord'."

**8. Clear pronouns at key actions.** "She picks up the RED FOLDER," not "She picks it up" after a list of objects.

**9. No shooting-script conventions; about a page a minute.** No scene numbers, OMITTED, revision marks or CONTINUED. Silent stretches play longer than they read. [general] Length by format:

| | Shorts | Features |
|---|---|---|
| Typical pages | 5–15; under ~10–12 programs most easily; 15–20 possible, past that needs a reason; credits count toward running time | 90–120; comedies toward 90–100; dramas may reach ~120; over 120 raise once |
| Page ≈ minute | yes; silent stretches play longer than they read | yes |
| Title page | title, by, name, contact; anonymous for blind competitions | same |

**10. Blind competitions need an anonymous title page.**

**11. Flashbacks and dreams are opened and closed.** Mark them in the heading (`INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT (FLASHBACK)`) and close them (`BACK TO PRESENT` / `END FLASHBACK`); multi-scene ones bracket BEGIN / END. Shorts: question whether the flashback should exist (note to Structure). [general]

**12. CONTINUOUS, LATER, SAME: one job each.** CONTINUOUS only when the flow isn't obvious; LATER for an unspecified gap; SAME for crosscuts. No stacked modifiers. Secondary headings (`KITCHEN`) only inside an established master location. [general]

**13. INSERT and SUPER.** SUPER text in quotes. INSERT returns with BACK TO SCENE. Prefer describing the object in action unless its text must be read. [general]

## Checklist
1. Complete headings, one separator style, consistent time-of-day style?
2. Caps and age at first appearance in action, and only then?
3. Cue names spelled consistently?
4. Correct extensions for unseen voices?
5. Parentheticals under about four words, not stage directions?
6. Each transition doing something a cut can't?
7. Montages and intercuts set up clearly?
8. Screen text word for word, one label style, no double colons?
9. Unambiguous pronouns at key actions?
10. No shooting-script marks?
11. Few camera directions, each carrying meaning?
12. Page count vs. running time; anonymous title page for blind competitions?
13. Flashbacks and dreams opened and closed?
14. Title page: title, by, name, contact, nothing else (no draft number, date or registration)?
15. One full pass for spelling, homophones and mixed tenses?

## Common mistakes
Copying production-draft formatting. Mixing up (V.O.) and (O.S.). Re-introducing characters in caps. Parentheticals that are stage directions. Missing (CONT'D)/(MORE) at page breaks (let software handle it). Shorts: running past 15–20 pages without a reason. Features: over 120 pages.
