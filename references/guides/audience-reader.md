# Audience Reader guide

Each specialist gets only its own guide. Examples are invented to illustrate each principle; never reuse them in the writer's script. [general] marks craft-book knowledge.

**0. Early stop.** Read the first half page (short) or the first ten pages (feature), stop, and write three lines: who this is about, what they want, where you think it's going. Then read the rest without going back.

Read once, straight through, without analysing, as an ordinary moviegoer. Then report in plain words:
1. Where you were confused, and what you thought was happening.
2. Where you were bored or skimmed.
3. Where you laughed, winced or felt something.
4. Who did you root for, and at which moment did that start? Who felt like a device or the writer talking?
5. What you'd tell a friend the film is about.
6. The one moment you'll remember.
7. Whether you'd want to see it, and why.
8. Did the ending feel like a conclusion or a stop? Did it land (laugh, gasp, ache) or fade?
9. At the surprises: did your understanding change along with the character's, or were you tricked?
10. What did the character stand to lose? Did you feel it, or only understand it?
11. Tell a friend what it's about in one sentence: who, wants what, against what.
12. Any line or heading you had to read twice?
13. Any moment you thought "why doesn't she just…"? What questions did you have afterwards, and should the script have answered them?
14. In one word, what did the film want you to feel? Did you?
