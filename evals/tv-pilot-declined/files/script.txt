COLD OPEN

EXT. KESTREL POINT HARBOR - BREAKWATER - PRE-DAWN

Black water. A harbor light turns on a steel tower, red,
then nothing, then red.

A fishing boat, the MARGARET ANNE, idles in against the
breakwater with no running lights. Two figures on deck.

HARBORMASTER LENA KOSKI (41), oilskin over pajamas, boots
unlaced, watches from the top of the breakwater with a
flashlight she hasn't switched on.

The boat ties off. One figure climbs the ladder. The other
stays with the engine.

The climber reaches the top: CAL DUFRESNE (29), a bag over
his shoulder, a face he'd rather she didn't see.

                    LENA
          You're not on the log, Cal.

                    CAL
          Engine trouble. Had to come in.

                    LENA
          Engine sounds fine.

                    CAL
          It does now.

She clicks the flashlight on. Points it at the bag, not at
him.

                    LENA
          What's in it?

                    CAL
          Lena.

                    LENA
          I'm asking as the harbormaster.

                    CAL
          Then as the harbormaster, you didn't
          see me.

He walks past her. She lets him. The flashlight follows the
bag until it's gone.

She looks down at the boat. The second figure looks up. In
the red of the harbor light, for a second: a woman's face,
grey hair, a face Lena knows.

Then dark.

                    LENA
                (quiet)
          Mum?

The light comes round again. The deck is empty.

MAIN TITLES.

END OF COLD OPEN


ACT ONE

INT. KOSKI HOUSE - KITCHEN - MORNING

A kitchen that was renovated once, in 1988. Lena, dressed
now, pours coffee into a travel mug and misses.

Her son, NIKO KOSKI (16), eats cereal standing up, school
bag already on.

                    NIKO
          You were out.

                    LENA
          Boat came in off the log.

                    NIKO
          Whose?

                    LENA
          Nobody's.

                    NIKO
          That's not a boat.

She wipes the counter. Doesn't answer.

                    NIKO (CONT'D)
          Dad called. He wants to know if I'm
          coming for the summer.

                    LENA
          It's February.

                    NIKO
          He plans ahead. It's his whole thing.

                    LENA
          Tell him we'll talk about it.

                    NIKO
          I told him you'd say that.

He leaves. She stands with the cloth in her hand, looking at
the window. The harbor light is off now. Daylight.

INT. HARBORMASTER'S OFFICE - DAY

A wooden hut on the quay. Charts, a VHF radio, a kettle. A
logbook open on the desk.

Lena's deputy, DESMOND ACHEBE (58), reading glasses, a
cardigan, already there, already making tea.

                    DESMOND
          You want to tell me why the Margaret
          Anne's tied to the breakwater with no
          entry in the book?

                    LENA
          Cal had engine trouble.

                    DESMOND
          Cal's had engine trouble four
          Tuesdays running.

                    LENA
          Then he should get it looked at.

Desmond sets the tea down in front of her. Doesn't let go of
the handle.

                    DESMOND
          Lena. The new owner's coming Monday.
          Harbor Authority. Everything in the
          book or everything on us.

                    LENA
          I know what Monday is.

                    DESMOND
          Then write it in.

She picks up the pen. Holds it over the line. Doesn't write.

                    LENA
          Des. Who was crewing for Cal last
          night?

                    DESMOND
          He crews alone. Always has.

                    LENA
          Not last night.

Desmond takes his glasses off. Looks at her properly.

                    DESMOND
          Who'd you see?

A long beat.

                    LENA
          Nobody. It was dark.

She writes in the book: MARGARET ANNE - 04:40 - ENGINE. Her
handwriting is steady. Her other hand isn't.

EXT. KESTREL POINT - MAIN STREET - DAY

A one-street town. Lena walks past the chandlery, the
closed cafe, the open bar.

Across the street, a black SUV with out-of-town plates.
A WOMAN IN A SUIT (40s), MARGOT HALE, on the phone, watching
the harbor. She sees Lena. Raises a hand, friendly. Lena
nods back. Keeps walking.

                    MARGOT
                (into phone, as Lena passes)
          No, she's exactly who the file says
          she is. That's the problem.

EXT. KESTREL POINT HARBOR - BREAKWATER - DAY

The Margaret Anne, in daylight. Lena climbs down onto the
deck. Nobody aboard.

She opens the wheelhouse door. On the chart table: a mug,
still warm. Beside it, a hair clip. Tortoiseshell. Old.

She picks it up. Turns it over. On the back, scratched into
the plastic with a pin: A.K.

Lena closes her hand around it.

Behind her, on the quay, the harbor light clicks on in broad
daylight. Red. Then nothing. Then red.

She turns and looks at it.

                    LENA
          That's not supposed to do that.

END OF ACT ONE
