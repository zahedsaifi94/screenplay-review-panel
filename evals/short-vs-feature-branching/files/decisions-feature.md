## Goals
Format: feature. Form: classical (closed). Logline: A night porter who keeps everything in order covers up a new colleague's mistake on the same night his daughter is admitted to his own hospital, and has to choose between the lie that protects them both and the truth that costs him the job. Length ceiling: 100 pages. Tone: quiet, contained drama; one night and one morning. Targets: screenplay competitions in the spring (drama category). Production scale: one hospital location, small cast, could be made low budget.

## Intent (orchestrator only)
Jonah fixes what is in front of him so he never has to look at what's behind him. The cover-up for Marisol and the missed signs with Tess are the same habit. He gives up the job because it's the only way to stop fixing and start looking.
- Controlling idea: honesty prevails because he finally chooses to look at what he's been avoiding. Counter-idea: protecting people from consequences is also a kind of care.
- Intended effect: recognition.

## Deliberate choices (don't flag)
1. Arthur Pell dies; the script never settles whether the delay caused it.
2. Tess's overdose is paracetamol, deliberate, and the script does not explain why beyond what Priya says.
3. The ending is open on whether Jonah gets another job; it is closed on the lie.
